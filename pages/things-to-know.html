Getting around: transport in Ghana

Ghana offers many ways to travel, from shared minibuses to flights. Distances can be longer than they look on a map, and traffic in Accra and Kumasi can be heavy.

Typical journey: Accra to Kumasi is a few hours by road, and Accra to Cape Coast is about two to three hours, depending on traffic.

Weather from January to December

Southern Ghana has two rainy seasons, the main one from about April to July and a shorter one around September to November. Northern Ghana has one rainy season, roughly May to October, and a long dry season. Temperatures stay warm all year.

Money: we spend cedis in Ghana

The currency of Ghana is the Ghanaian cedi (code GHS, often written GH¢ or GH₵). One cedi is made up of 100 pesewas. Everyday spending in shops, markets, transport and restaurants is done in cedis.

Cash (cedis)

Carry small notes and coins for trotros, taxis, markets and tips. Vendors may not have change for large notes.

Foreign currency

US dollars, British pounds and euros can be exchanged for cedis at banks and licensed foreign exchange bureaus. Foreign cash is not normally used for everyday purchases. Some hotels and tour operators quote prices in dollars.

Bank cards

Visa and Mastercard are accepted at many hotels, restaurants and shops in cities. Use ATMs to withdraw cedis. Tell your bank you are travelling.

Mobile money

Mobile money services are widely used for payments. Visitors may be able to use them with a local SIM and ID. Ask your host or hotel for help.

Exchange rates and rules change. Check current rates and any limits before you travel.

Languages spoken in Ghana

Ghana is known as an English-speaking country because English is the official language, used in government, schools and business. But Ghana is also home to dozens of local languages, and many people speak more than one. Which language dominates depends on where you are.

    A few friendly words in Twi: Akwaaba (welcome), Maakye (good morning) and Medaase (thank you).
